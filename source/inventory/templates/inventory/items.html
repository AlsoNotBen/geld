The database holds one table for every kind of item: an asset, a stock item, a consumable or a component. The page holds one form for the same reason, and a select sets the type. A relationship joins two items: a parent and a child, of one of five types, with a quantity. The page shows the relationships of the selected item at the right, and a small form adds one more. The editor is hidden until New Item (?new=1) is clicked, or an item is selected in the table (?item=pk). It reuses .bom with two columns: left the form of the item, in three tabs: Manufacturer, Model (with its Category) and Item. Each tab has a select, a search button and a "+" button that shows the fields of a new entry. right the relationships of the selected item, and the form that adds one. A note names the one rule that the middleware checks: a relationship must not close a circle. The view gives the items of the table (?q, ?item_type), the selected item (?item=pk) and its relationships. Without a selection, the form makes a new item. The quantity and the notes have no model field yet, and ItemForm ignores these fields. ============================================================================ {% endcomment %} {% block page_key %}items{% endblock %} {% block title %}Items · Inventory · Console{% endblock %} {% block content %}

Items{% notes_pill %}

An item is a row of one kind: an asset, a stock item, a consumable or a component. The type of an item does not change what an item is made of; a relationship does that. A relationship joins two items, a parent and a child, with a type and a quantity. A bill of materials, an accessory, a replacement and a compatible part are all the same kind of row, with a different type. Select an item in the table below to edit it, or to change what it relates to.
